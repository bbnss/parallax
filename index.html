For the West, it is framed as a test of global adherence to international law and free trade; for the East, it appears as a series of high-stakes policy announcements and humanitarian incidents; for the Middle East, it is viewed through the lens of regional autonomy against external interference; and for Russia, it serves as a prime example of Western attempts at economic control over vital global arteries. The Strait of Hormuz thus becomes less a shipping lane and more a fault line upon which competing world orders are being tested. In this volatile crucible, energy security is merely the commodity traded by great power ambitions.
